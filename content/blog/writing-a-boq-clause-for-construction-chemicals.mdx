A BOQ clause for a construction chemical has to let a tenderer price the right product at the right quantity without a phone call. It states supply and apply, the product and the manufacturer, the classification to the standard, the consumption rate per square metre as the data sheet publishes it, the thickness or the number of coats, the mixing ratio, the pack size, preparation and application to the manufacturer's current data sheet, and that the rate excludes wastage. This guide covers each element, how a quantity surveyor checks the rate, the difference between a performance clause and a named product clause, and two complete sample clauses built from Mapei data sheets.

## What must a BOQ clause for construction chemicals say?

A clause is a contract. Every word in it either tells the tenderer what to price or leaves a gap the tenderer fills with the cheapest assumption. The elements, in the order a clause usually runs:

1. **Supply and apply.** Or supply only, if the applicator is a separate contract. State which.
2. **Product name and manufacturer.** Exactly as the data sheet prints them, so the submittal and the delivery note match the clause.
3. **Classification.** The class to the standard: ISO 13007 or EN 12004 for tile adhesives, EN 13813 for levelling compounds, EN 14891 for membranes under tiles, EN 1504 for concrete repair. An alternative is judged against it.
4. **Consumption rate per square metre.** From the data sheet, stated per millimetre of thickness where the product is applied in a bed, per coat where it is applied as a coating.
5. **Thickness or coats.** The design thickness or the number of coats, within the range the data sheet permits.
6. **Mixing.** The ratio, so the site cannot thin a product to make it go further.
7. **Pack size.** So the tenderer converts the rate to packs and the delivery is checked against the clause.
8. **Preparation and application to the manufacturer's current technical data sheet.** This single sentence imports the whole data sheet into the contract.
9. **Wastage excluded.** The rate is a theoretical figure. The contractor prices wastage in the rate or in a separate line, never by inflating the quantity.

A clause that carries all nine is one a consultant can approve, a QS can check and a supplier can price. A clause that carries three of them is a dispute waiting for the final account.

## Which layers does one clause cover?

One clause, one layer. ## What unit of measurement does the clause carry?

Square metres for anything applied over an area: primers, adhesives, membranes, levelling compounds, coatings and grouts. Linear metres for sealants, tapes, profiles and joint fillers. Kilograms or litres only where there is no area to measure, such as admixtures dosed by cement weight.

Measure the net area from the drawings and let the consumption rate convert it to packs. Do not measure in bags: 300 bags of adhesive commits to a thickness nobody wrote down, and when the screed arrives 3 mm low there is no way to adjust it. 850 m2 at 5 mm at the data sheet rate is adjusted by changing one number.

## How does a quantity surveyor check the rate?

The check is three steps and takes a minute per clause with the data sheet open.

**Step 1.** Area in square metres, multiplied by thickness in millimetres, multiplied by the consumption in kilograms per square metre per millimetre, gives the mass.

**Step 2.** Mass divided by the pack size gives packs. Round up.

**Step 3.** Compare to the quantity in the bid and ask about the difference.

A worked example for the levelling compound in the model. The floor is 850 m2, the design thickness is 5 mm, and the product is [Ultraplan Eco 20](/brands/mapei/self-leveling-and-microcement/ultraplan-eco-20), whose data sheet states 1.6 kg/m2 per millimetre of thickness and a 23 kg bag.

850 m2 multiplied by 5 mm is 4,250. Multiplied by 1.6 kg is 6,800 kg. Divided by 23 kg is 295.65, so 296 bags.

If the bid shows 340 bags, that is 44 bags or 15 percent over the data sheet figure: wastage priced into the quantity, which the clause forbids, a rate from a different product, or a thicker pour than the clause states. Each is a question to ask before the award, not after.

The same arithmetic for a tile adhesive, if the floor were tiled: 850 m2 at a 5 mm bed with [Keraflex Maxi S1 Zero](/brands/mapei/tile-adhesives-and-grouts/keraflex-maxi-s1-zero), 1.2 kg/m2 per millimetre in a 25 kg bag, is 850 by 5 by 1.2, which is 5,100 kg, divided by 25, exactly 204 bags.

Every product page carries a consumption calculator that runs this arithmetic from the transcribed sheet, so the QS check is a comparison. Where the manufacturer publishes no rate, the page says so and the clause refers the tenderer to the data sheet on the product page.

## Performance clause or named product with or equal and approved?

A **performance clause** describes what the product must do and names no manufacturer: "cementitious tile adhesive, improved, slip resistant, extended open time, deformable, class C2 TE S1 to ISO 13007-1". Any product that holds that class can be offered. It suits public tenders and projects that want competition between manufacturers. Its weakness is that it cannot carry a rate or a pack size, because those belong to a product, so the quantity is stated in square metres with the thickness and nothing else.

A **named product clause** fixes the benchmark: "Keraflex Maxi S1 Zero manufactured by Mapei, or equal and approved". The named product supplies the rate, the thickness range, the mixing ratio and the pack size, so the clause is complete and the quantity can be checked. The words "or equal and approved" let a tenderer propose an alternative, judged against the classification in the clause through the submittal. Without the classification, "equal" has no meaning.

In the UAE a C2 TE S1 clause admits several products Lapiz Blue supplies: Keraflex Maxi S1 Zero at ISO 13007-1 C2TE S1, [H40 Gel](/brands/kerakoll/tile-adhesives-and-grouts/h40-gel) from the [Kerakoll hub](/brands/kerakoll) at C2 TE S1, and Pegoland Flex from Pidilite Puma at C2 TE S1. Writing the class into the clause gives the project three priced routes to the same performance.

## Sample clause: a self levelling compound

Built from the Ultraplan Eco 20 data sheet as the product page transcribes it, in the form the BOQ clause builder on that page produces:

> Supply and apply Ultraplan Eco 20, a self levelling compound manufactured by Mapei, classified EXT EN 13813 CT C30 F7 A2FL S1. Allow 1.6 kg/m2 per mm of thickness, as published by the manufacturer. Applied thickness per layer from 1 to 10 mm; design thickness 5 mm. Mix at 24 to 25 parts of water per 100 parts by weight of powder. Supplied in 23 kg bags. Substrate to be primed with the manufacturer's primer for the substrate. Prepare the substrate and apply strictly in accordance with the manufacturer's current technical data sheet. Rates are for estimating and exclude wastage. Unit of measurement: m2.

Every figure in it is on the [Ultraplan Eco 20](/brands/mapei/self-leveling-and-microcement/ultraplan-eco-20) page, and the other compounds in the [Mapei self levelling and microcement](/brands/mapei/self-leveling-and-microcement) range follow the same form with their own rates. The primer is a separate clause, as the model shows.

## Sample clause: a tile adhesive

Built the same way from the Keraflex Maxi S1 Zero page:

> Supply and apply Keraflex Maxi S1 Zero, a tile adhesive manufactured by Mapei, classified ISO 13007-1 C2TE S1. Allow 1.2 kg/m2 per mm of thickness, as published by the manufacturer. Design bed thickness 5 mm under a notched trowel, back buttered for tiles above 600 by 600 mm. Mix at 100 parts by weight of powder with 28 to 30 parts of water. Supplied in 25 kg bags. Prepare the substrate and apply strictly in accordance with the manufacturer's current technical data sheet. Rates are for estimating and exclude wastage. Unit of measurement: m2.

The design bed thickness and the back buttering are the specifier's additions; the builder prints only what the sheet states. Where an alternative is allowed, add "or equal and approved" after the manufacturer's name and keep the classification in the clause.

## How does the BOQ clause builder on each product page work?

Every product page on this site has a BOQ clause panel. It reads the transcribed data sheet and writes a clause one sentence at a time: the product, the manufacturer and the type; the classification if the sheet states one; the consumption rate if the manufacturer publishes one; the thickness, the mixing ratio and the pack size if the sheet gives them; and the closing sentence on preparation, the data sheet and wastage. A sentence is written only where the value exists, because the text is going into a tender document.

The panel has three outputs. **Copy** puts the clause on the clipboard as one paragraph for pasting into a specification. **CSV** downloads the same data as field and value rows, including set time, pot life and open time where the sheet gives them, and the unit of measurement, for a BOQ spreadsheet. **TXT** downloads the paragraph as a plain text file for a document management system.

The clause uses the same rate as the page's calculator, so the two can never disagree, and when the data sheet is revised the next clause copied carries the new figure.

## What about wastage, preparation and the items the clause excludes?

Three things stay outside the product clause and need a home elsewhere in the BOQ.

**Wastage** is the contractor's risk, priced in the rate or carried as a stated percentage in a separate line. It is never added to the measured quantity, which then no longer matches the drawings and cannot be remeasured.

**Preparation** of the substrate, grinding, cleaning, moisture testing, crack repair and priming, is measured separately. The product clause imports the data sheet's requirements as a condition, but the work of meeting them is its own item.

**Accessories and ancillaries** that the system needs but the data sheet does not quantify, tapes at upstands, movement joint profiles, spacers, are measured in linear metres or by number in their own clauses.
