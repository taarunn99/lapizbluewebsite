An R4 repair mortar is for structural repairs where the mortar must share load with the parent concrete: columns, beams, slab soffits, car park decks and any member the engineer has designed. An R3 mortar is the structural class for lower strength concrete and for general spall patching where the mortar is asked to do no more than the original concrete did. Both are defined by EN 1504-3, both go on only after breakout to sound concrete, cleaning and priming of the bar and a bonding coat where the sheet asks for one, and both are finished with a fairing coat and a protective coating. This guide walks through that sequence and the choice at each step.

## What do the R classes in EN 1504-3 mean?

EN 1504 is the European standard for the protection and repair of concrete structures. Part 3 covers repair mortars and sorts them into four classes. R1 and R2 are non structural: they restore profile and cover but are not counted on to carry load. R3 and R4 are structural. Each class sets minimum compressive strength, bond to the substrate, modulus of elasticity, restrained shrinkage behaviour and carbonation resistance, and R4 is the higher class on every count.

This gives the specifier one line. Write "repair mortar to EN 1504-3 class R4" and any product with that CE mark qualifies; add the product name and the applicator knows what to price. On the [Mapei](/brands/mapei) side the class is printed on every page in the [concrete additives and repair systems](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems) line, for the quantity surveyor to check against the clause.

Part 3 does not act alone. Part 7 covers the corrosion protection on the bar, Part 4 structural bonding, Part 2 the protective coating, and Part 10 site application. A method statement that cites them all is one a consultant signs quickly.

## Why does the repair start with breakout and not with mortar?

A spall is a symptom. The cause is almost always reinforcement that has corroded, expanded and pushed the cover off, or a defect such as honeycombing that let water in. Fill the hole without dealing with the cause and the patch falls out within two or three summers.

So the first step after the survey is breakout. Saw cut the outline at least 10 mm deep as a square or rectangle, never a feathered edge, because thin mortar at a feathered edge is the first part to debond. Break out inside the line until sound concrete is reached: dense, uncracked and ringing under a hammer. Where a corroded bar is exposed, continue at least 20 mm behind it and along it until the steel is clean on both sides of the damaged length.

### Cover meter and chloride testing in brief

Two tests show how far the problem extends beyond what is visible. A cover meter survey maps the depth of the bar and shows where low cover has left it within reach of carbonation or chlorides. Dust samples drilled at depth increments and tested for chloride show whether chlorides from sea air, ground or the original mix have reached the bar; above the engineer's threshold, quoted as a percentage of cement weight, the concrete around that bar comes out even where it looks sound. These results set the repair areas, and they are why repair quantities stay provisional until breakout is complete.

## How do you clean and prime the reinforcement?

Once exposed, the bar is cleaned to bright metal by grit blasting or, on small repairs, wire brushing and needle gunning. Rust left on the bar carries chlorides and goes on corroding under the new mortar. Where a bar has lost more section than the engineer allows, often set at 10 to 20 per cent, it is lapped or replaced, and that decision belongs to the engineer.

The clean bar is then coated with a corrosion protection product classified under EN 1504-7. [Mapefer](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/mapefer) is the Mapei cementitious anti corrosion coating for this step, two coats by brush along the full exposed length and lapped onto the concrete. Kerakoll pairs its repair system with [Geolite Magma](/brands/kerakoll/grouts-and-concrete-repair/geolite-magma), classified under EN 1504-7, from its [grouts and concrete repair](/brands/kerakoll/grouts-and-concrete-repair) line. Place the mortar within the window the primer sheet states; left too long, the primer becomes a bond breaker.

## Bonding slurry or no bonding slurry?

The model shows the bar primer and the bonding slurry as one thin layer because they are applied in the same visit. Whether the concrete face needs a bonding coat at all is a data sheet question, and the answer differs between products.

Traditional practice brushes a slurry of the repair mortar gauged with an SBR latex such as [Planicrete](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/planicrete) onto the saturated, surface dry concrete and places the mortar while it is wet. Many current R4 mortars bond directly to a saturated, surface dry substrate and the sheet says to omit the slurry; a slurry that dries before the mortar arrives is a weaker plane than no slurry at all. Where the engineer requires a structural bond under EN 1504-4, for example a formed repair to a loaded column, a two part epoxy such as [Eporip A+B](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/eporip-a-b) is brushed on and the mortar placed while it is tacky.

## Thixotropic or flowable: how do you choose the mortar's form?

Thixotropic mortars hold their shape. They are trowelled or sprayed onto vertical faces and soffits in layers, typically 10 to 40 mm a layer within the limits on the sheet, and stay where they are put. This is the form for most hand repairs to columns, beams, parapets and soffits. [Mapegrout T60 ME](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/mapegrout-t60-me) and [Mapegrout TX 60 ME](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/mapegrout-tx-60-me) are the Mapei R4 thixotropic mortars made for the Middle East; [weberep 331 TX](/brands/weber/repair-and-anchoring/weberep-331-tx) is the thixotropic grade in the Weber [repair and anchoring](/brands/weber/repair-and-anchoring) line.

Flowable mortars are poured into shuttering: the full face of a column, the underside of a beam cast through a letterbox, a deep repair that cannot be compacted by hand. Where the repair is deeper than a neat mortar allows, the same product is extended with a graded aggregate such as [Mapegrout Aggregate](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/mapegrout-aggregate) at the ratio on the sheet.

A third form is the fast setting mortar for roads, manholes and areas that must reopen in hours: [Mapegrout SV ME](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/mapegrout-sv-me) in class R4 and [Mapegrout Rapido](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/mapegrout-rapido) in class R3.

## R3 or R4: where is each used on UAE structures?

Specify R4 where the engineer has designed the repair as structural on a primary element: columns and shear walls in a tower car park, transfer beams, post tensioned soffits, bridge and marine structures. [Planitop HPC](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/planitop-hpc) is the R4 choice for a very high strength, fibre reinforced pour. Kerakoll's [Geolite](/brands/kerakoll/grouts-and-concrete-repair/geolite) is classified under EN 1504-3 and is the one bag the Kerakoll system uses for the repair, the fairing and the finish.

Specify R3 for structural repairs to ordinary members in lower strength concrete, for example a 1990s villa compound or a warehouse frame cast at 25 to 30 N/mm2, where an R4 mortar would be far stiffer than the concrete around it and the mismatch does nothing useful. R3 also suits widespread spall patching to balconies, parapets and boundary walls where the bar is corroded but the member is lightly loaded. [Planitop 400 ME](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/planitop-400-me) is the Mapei R3 patch repair mortar for that work.

On a project with hundreds of small repairs one R4 mortar for everything is often simpler than managing two products. The consultant accepts R4 wherever R3 is specified; the reverse is never true.

### Fairing coat and anti carbonation coating

A repair mortar is not a finish. Once cured, the whole face of the element, repairs and original concrete together, is skimmed with a fairing coat 1 to 3 mm thick to close the pores and level the surface. [Mapefinish](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/mapefinish) and [Planitop Raso Max](/brands/mapei/concrete-additives-and-repair-systems/planitop-raso-max), the latter classified R2 under EN 1504-3, are the common choices. Over that goes a protective coating classified under EN 1504-2, covering the whole element because its job is to slow carbonation and chloride ingress into the original concrete that was not broken out. [Elastocolor Paint](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/elastocolor-paint) is an elastomeric anti carbonation paint classified under EN 1504-2 for principles PI, MC and IR; in the Kerakoll system [Geolite Microsilicato](/brands/kerakoll/surface-treatments/geolite-microsilicato), also classified under EN 1504-2, is the matching finish. Where epoxy repair is specified instead of cementitious work, the [Pidilite Puma epoxy repair](/brands/pidilite-puma/epoxy-repair) line covers it.

## How much mortar for twelve spalls?

Take a car park soffit with 12 spalls averaging 0.3 m2, broken out to an average depth of 40 mm. The repair mortar has a wet density of roughly 2,000 kg per m3; the exact yield on the product sheet replaces that figure.

Step 1. Area: 12 × 0.3 = 3.6 m2.

Step 2. Volume: 3.6 × 0.040 = 0.144 m3.

Step 3. Mass: 0.144 × 2,000 = 288 kg.

Step 4. Wastage. Hand application to a soffit drops material and the breakout is never exactly 40 mm. Allow 10 per cent: 288 × 1.10 = 316.8 kg, call it 320 kg.

Step 5. Bags. If the mortar comes in 25 kg bags, 320 ÷ 25 = 12.8, so 13 bags. Check the bag size on the product page.

Step 6. Bar primer and bonding coat are small quantities here; the fairing coat and the coating are quoted for the whole soffit, not the 3.6 m2 of repairs, and that is usually the larger line on the quote.

## What does the method statement contain?

A concrete repair method statement a UAE consultant signs without a second round covers, in order: the survey and the marking of repair areas; saw cutting and breakout to sound concrete with the 20 mm rule behind the bar; cleaning the steel and the criterion for lapping or replacing it; the EN 1504-7 primer and its recoat window; wetting the substrate to saturated surface dry; the bonding coat, or the sheet's instruction to omit it; the repair mortar with its class, layer limits and curing, which in UAE heat means shading, wet hessian or a curing membrane for the period the sheet states; the fairing coat; the EN 1504-2 coating and its coverage; and the inspection points, usually a hammer survey of each repair and pull off tests on a sample.
