A consultant submittal pack for construction chemicals needs six things: the technical data sheet and the safety data sheet for every product in the system, an authorisation letter from the manufacturer naming Lapiz Blue General Trading LLC as the appointed distributor, a method statement for the system, the test certificates or declarations of performance behind the classification each product claims, a compliance statement that sets each specification clause against the data sheet value, and samples where the consultant asks for them. The pack covers the whole system, not one product. It is signed by the contractor, supported by the distributor and approved by the consultant, and this guide sets out each item and why packs come back rejected.

## What goes in a submittal pack for construction chemicals?

A material submittal is the contractor's formal proposal to use a named product in place of the words in the specification. The consultant checks that the product does what the clause asked for, that it is genuine, that the supply chain is real and that the site knows how to install it. Each document answers one of those questions.

| Document | What it proves | Who produces it |
|---|---|---|
| Technical data sheet | The product does what the clause asks | Manufacturer, via the product page |
| Safety data sheet | The site can handle and store it | Manufacturer, on request |
| Authorisation letter | The supplier is the appointed distributor | Manufacturer, naming Lapiz Blue General Trading LLC |
| Method statement | The site knows the sequence and the limits | Contractor, built on the manufacturer's sheet |
| Test certificates | The classification is tested, not claimed | Manufacturer or notified body |
| Compliance statement | Clause by clause, the product meets the spec | Contractor |
| Samples | The colour, texture or pack is what was approved | Distributor |

The pack is one bundle per system: a tiled floor is primer, adhesive and grout; a wet area adds the membrane and the tape. A consultant who receives three separate adhesive submittals for one floor sends them all back.

## Why does the consultant want the technical data sheet and the safety data sheet?

The technical data sheet is the only document in the pack that the manufacturer stands behind. It carries the classification, the consumption rate, the thickness range, the mixing ratio, the pot life, the open time and the pack size. Every number in the method statement and the compliance statement has to trace back to it. The consultant checks the issue date, so the sheet must be the current edition. Every Lapiz Blue product page carries the current technical data sheet as a PDF, the same file we send with the quotation.

The safety data sheet is a legal document, not a technical one. It tells the site how to store, handle and dispose of the product and what to do in an incident. UAE main contractors' HSE departments will not accept a material on site without it, so a submittal without an SDS is incomplete even when the technical case is perfect. It is sent on request from the product page with the submittal pack.

## What must the distributor authorisation letter say?

The letter proves the supply chain. It must be on the manufacturer's letterhead, signed, dated within a period the consultant accepts, and it must name the distributor by its exact legal name: Lapiz Blue General Trading LLC. A letter that names a group, a sister company or a shortened trading name does not match the company on the purchase order and the delivery note, and a careful consultant will reject it for that reason alone.

Lapiz Blue is appointed in writing by Mapei, Kerakoll, Weber Saint-Gobain, Laticrete, Pidilite Puma and AkzoNobel. The letters are published on the [credentials page](/credentials) so a consultant can check them without asking, and the current letter is attached to every submittal pack request. Where a project needs a letter addressed to it by name, say so and the manufacturer's regional office issues it.

## What do the method statement, test certificates and samples prove?

The method statement shows the consultant that the site understands the system. It names the substrate condition, the preparation, each product with its thickness and its drying or open time in the expected site temperature, the joint width, the curing and the protection. It is the contractor's document, because the contractor is responsible for the installation, but every limit in it comes from the data sheets. Lapiz Blue supplies method statement templates for the common systems across the [Mapei hub](/brands/mapei) range, which the contractor edits to the project.

Test certificates and declarations of performance prove the classification. A data sheet that says C2 TE S1 is a claim; a declaration of performance from the manufacturer, or a certificate from a notified body, is the evidence. Consultants on government, healthcare and hospitality projects in the UAE ask for them as a rule, and the manufacturer provides them for products that carry CE or EN classifications.

Samples settle what paper cannot: grout colour against the approved tile, the texture of a self levelling finish, the pack and label the site will receive. Label the sample with the product, the batch and the submittal reference, and keep the approved sample on site.

## How do you write the compliance statement against the specification clause?

The compliance statement is a table with three columns: the specification clause word for word, the proposed product's value from the data sheet, and the verdict. Writing "complies" in the third column without filling the second is the single most common reason a pack comes back.

A worked example for one clause of a tiled floor:

| Specification clause | Proposed product | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Tile adhesive: cementitious, improved, slip resistant, extended open time, deformable, class C2 TE S1 to ISO 13007-1 | Keraflex Maxi S1 Zero, classified ISO 13007-1 C2TE S1, open time over 30 minutes per the data sheet | Complies |
| Grout: cementitious, improved, fast setting, water repellent, class CG2 FWA | Ultracolor Plus, classified CG2FWA | Complies |
| Primer: water based acrylic for absorbent cementitious substrates | Primer G, water based acrylic primer, consumption per the data sheet | Complies |

Where the product exceeds the clause, say so with the value. Where it meets the clause by a different route, for example an ANSI classification against an EN clause, say "complies, by equivalence" and attach the test. Where it does not meet the clause, say so and propose the product that does. A consultant trusts a statement that admits one gap over one that claims twelve passes.

## Who signs what?

The contractor signs the transmittal and the compliance statement, because the contractor proposes the material and carries the installation warranty, and issues the method statement with the applicator's input.

The distributor signs the authorisation confirmation, the stock and lead time confirmation, and the covering letter that lists the documents in the pack. Lapiz Blue issues these on its own letterhead, naming the project and the consultant.

The manufacturer signs nothing project specific unless asked. Its signature is the published data sheet, the declaration of performance and the distributor letter. Where a project warranty is required, the regional office issues it after the method statement is approved.

The consultant signs the approval stamp: approved, approved as noted, revise and resubmit, or rejected. "Approved as noted" is an approval with conditions, and the conditions go into the method statement before work starts.

## Why do submittals get rejected?

In order of frequency, from the packs we have helped to resubmit:

1. An out of date technical data sheet, usually one saved from a previous project.
2. A classification that does not match: C2 TE offered against C2 TE S1, or CG2 against CG2 WA.
3. The right manufacturer and the wrong product: a rapid setting adhesive offered because it was in stock, against a clause asking for extended open time.
4. No safety data sheet, or a safety data sheet in a language the HSE department will not accept.
5. An authorisation letter for a different company, an expired letter, or a photocopy of a letter addressed to another project.
6. A compliance statement that says "complies" throughout with no data sheet values.
7. A pack for one product when the clause describes a system.
8. A method statement copied from the data sheet with no site specific temperatures, substrate or sequence.

Each of these is a resubmission cycle, and in a UAE programme that is a week to ten days. Getting the pack right first time is the cheapest thing a procurement team does all month.
