A basement is waterproofed from the outside against the water, a podium is waterproofed on top under a screed and a finish, and both are compared on the same five points: which side of the structure the membrane sits, whether it is bitumen, polyurethane or cementitious, how the construction joints are sealed, how the membrane is protected before backfill or screed, and what the consultant needs in the submittal before any of it is approved. This guide sets the options side by side for a UAE project, names the products Lapiz Blue stocks for each, lists the documents a submittal is returned without, and ends with the quantity for a 20 by 12 metre basement.

## Positive side or negative side: which does the structure need?

Positive side waterproofing goes on the face the water reaches first: the outside of the basement wall, under the raft, the top of the podium slab. Water pressure pushes the membrane onto the concrete, every membrane type can be used, and the structure itself stays dry. It is the default for new work because the outside face is accessible before backfill. The model below shows this arrangement from the inside out: reinforced concrete wall, primer, membrane, protection board, backfill.

Negative side waterproofing goes on the inside face, and it is chosen when the outside cannot be reached: an existing basement, a lift pit, a retaining wall cast against a neighbour's structure. Water pressure now pushes the membrane off the wall, so a bitumen or polyurethane coating will blister and detach. The products that work there are cementitious osmotic coatings that bond into the concrete: [Planiseal 88](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/planiseal-88), classified under EN 1504-2 for principles MC and IR, and Kerakoll's [Metric Osmotic](/brands/kerakoll/waterproofing/metric-osmotic), also EN 1504-2. Both need a sound, open textured surface, and neither bridges a live crack, so joints and cracks are sealed first.

Where groundwater is high the consultant will often ask for both: a positive side membrane as the primary barrier and a negative side osmotic coating in the pits and lowest rooms as a second line.

## Bitumen, polyurethane or cementitious: how do the options compare?

Bitumen is the cold applied economy option. [weberdry Bitumen](/brands/weber/waterproofing/weberdry-bitumen) and Mapei's [Plastimul DPM](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/plastimul-dpm) are brushed or sprayed emulsions for damp proof membranes on walls and under slabs, and [Plastimul DPC](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/plastimul-dpc) is the damp proof course at the base of a wall. They go over a bitumen primer such as [weberdry Prime SB](/brands/weber/waterproofing/weberdry-prime-sb) or [weberdry Prime WB](/brands/weber/waterproofing/weberdry-prime-wb) and they suit a dry site with low head. Where a sheet is specified, [Mapeproof AL](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/mapeproof-al) is a self adhesive membrane classified to EN 13967, with [Mapeproof AL Tape D10](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/mapeproof-al-tape-d10) for the laps and details.

Polyurethane is the seamless elastic option. [weberdry 360 PU](/brands/weber/waterproofing/weberdry-360-pu) is a liquid applied polyurethane membrane that cures to a continuous elastic film, follows falls and upstands without a lap, and bridges the movement a podium sees. Mapei's spray applied [Purtop 500 N](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/purtop-500-n) carries an EN 1504-2 coating classification and is used where a large podium or roof has to be covered quickly. Polyurethane wants a dry primed substrate, and the wet film thickness is measured as it goes down.

Cementitious is the option that tolerates a damp substrate and takes a bonded finish. [Mapelastic Foundation](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/mapelastic-foundation) is the two component flexible cementitious membrane made for foundations and below ground walls, classified CM O1, and the data sheet gives the hydrostatic pressure it resists from each side. [Aquastop Flex](/brands/kerakoll/waterproofing/aquastop-flex) is the Kerakoll equivalent with an EN 1504-2 classification. Cementitious membranes take a screed or a tile adhesive directly, which is why they are the usual choice on a podium that will be tiled.

| System | Where it fits | What to check on the sheet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bitumen emulsion | Damp proofing, low head, dry site | Dry film thickness, number of coats |
| Self adhesive sheet | Flat walls and rafts | Lap width, primer, EN 13967 class |
| Polyurethane liquid | Podiums, movement, seamless detailing | Wet film thickness, substrate moisture limit |
| Flexible cementitious | Damp substrates, foundations, tiled podiums | EN 14891 class, pressure resistance, coats |
| Osmotic cementitious | Negative side, pits, existing basements | EN 1504-2 class, surface preparation |

## Why does the membrane need a protection board?

A membrane that passes inspection is damaged by the next thing that touches it: backfill stones driven in by the compactor, reinforcement dragged past, scaffolding struck, and in the UAE the sun, which degrades an exposed bitumen or polyurethane film in days. The protection board takes all of that. It goes on after the membrane has cured and been inspected, fixed as the membrane maker shows, and backfill is compacted in layers against the board. On a raft the equivalent is a blinding screed over the membrane before reinforcement is fixed, and on a podium it is a geotextile or a protection screed before the falls screed goes down.

The rule is simple: nothing is backfilled or covered until the membrane has been inspected, photographed and signed off, and no membrane is left exposed longer than its data sheet allows. The [Mapei waterproofing line](/brands/mapei/waterproofing) and the [Weber waterproofing line](/brands/weber/waterproofing) list the boards, primers and accessories stocked with each membrane.

## How are construction joints and waterstops detailed?

Water enters a basement at the joints, not through the slab: the kicker joint between raft and wall, the vertical joints between wall pours, and the pipe and duct penetrations. Each is detailed before the membrane, because the membrane alone cannot seal a joint that opens.

The joint seal is a hydrophilic swelling waterstop fixed to the centre of the first pour before the second is cast. [weberdry Swellbar Rb 20*10](/brands/weber/waterproofing/weberdry-swellbar-rb-20-10) is a 20 by 10 mm swelling bar fixed along the kicker and the wall joints, and [Mapeproof Swell](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/mapeproof-swell) is the gun applied swelling paste used where a bar will not follow the profile, around pipes and at junctions. Both expand on contact with water and seal the joint from inside the concrete; they are kept dry until the second pour, placed with the cover the data sheet asks for, and never used in a joint designed to move.

On the outside, the membrane is reinforced across every construction joint with a strip of fabric, [Mapetex Sel](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/mapetex-sel) for Mapelastic Foundation or [weberdry Fabric FX](/brands/weber/waterproofing/weberdry-fabric-fx) for the Weber systems, bedded in the first coat. Tie bolt holes are filled with a repair mortar before priming, and [Mapeproof Mastic](/brands/mapei/waterproofing/mapeproof-mastic) seals the pipe collars and the edge of a sheet membrane. Movement joints, designed to open and close, get a sealant from the [sealants and anchoring line](/brands/mapei/sealants-and-anchoring) over a backer rod, carried through the finish, and the membrane is looped, not stretched, across them.

## What is the podium deck build up from slab to finish?

A podium is a basement roof laid flat. The slab is cast to falls of about 1 in 80 towards the outlets so the membrane never holds standing water. The concrete is cured, dry to the membrane's limit, ground where it is rough, and primed. The membrane goes on in two coats or to the wet film thickness the sheet gives, carried up every upstand, kerb and column base and onto the outlet flanges. It is flood tested, photographed, then protected with a geotextile or a protection screed, with insulation and a drainage layer where the design calls for them.

The falls screed comes next, with a product such as [Topcem Pronto](/brands/mapei/self-leveling-and-microcement/topcem-pronto), classified C30 F6 under EN 13813, so its strength and drying time are known. Movement joints are cut through the screed and the finish at the bays the designer sets, and they stop at the protection layer, never through the membrane. The finish is a tile on a C2 TE S2 or S1 adhesive such as [webercol Flex](/brands/weber/tile-adhesives-and-grouts/webercol-flex), classified C2TES2, or [Keraflex Maxi S1 Zero](/brands/mapei/tile-adhesives-and-grouts/keraflex-maxi-s1-zero), classified ISO 13007-1 C2TE S1, or pavers on pedestals, or planting over a root barrier. In every case the membrane sits under the falls screed, so the falls, the outlets and the upstands are decided before the first coat is mixed.

## Where does the drainage go?

Waterproofing is the second line; the first is a route for the water to leave. Below ground that is a drainage board outside the protection board, a perforated land drain at footing level in gravel and geotextile, and a sump and pump where the drain cannot fall to a soakaway. Relieving the head on the membrane is the most effective thing a basement design can do. On a podium it is outlets at the low points of the falls, an overflow, a drainage layer under paving and planters, and gratings the facility team can lift. Every outlet is a membrane detail, carried onto the flange and clamped, and belongs in the method statement.

## How much membrane for a 20 by 12 m basement?

Take a basement 20 m by 12 m on plan with walls 3.5 m below ground, waterproofed on the positive side.

1. Wall perimeter: 2 × (20 + 12) = 64 m.
2. Wall area: 64 × 3.5 = 224 m2.
3. Raft area: 20 × 12 = 240 m2.
4. Total membrane area: 224 + 240 = 464 m2.
5. Liquid or cementitious membrane: 464 m2 × the rate on the data sheet, in kg per m2 per coat, × the number of coats, plus 10 per cent for a rough surface and the strips over the joints. The sheet, not this example, sets the kit count.
6. Sheet membrane: 464 × 1.10 for side and end laps = 510.4, so 511 m2, divided by the roll area on the data sheet, rounded up.
7. Protection board: 224 m2 for the walls. The raft is protected by the blinding screed.
8. Swelling waterstop: kicker joint 64 m, plus wall construction joints. With two vertical joints in each 20 m wall and one in each 12 m wall, that is 6 × 3.5 = 21 m. Total 85 m; order 90 m for laps.
9. Primer: 464 m2 at the rate on the primer page.
