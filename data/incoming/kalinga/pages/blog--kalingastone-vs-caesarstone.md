# KalingaStone vs Caesarstone: The UAE Comparison
![Two white quartz slab samples compared side by side against a wall](/_next/image?url=%2Fblog-images%2Fkalingastone-vs-caesarstone.webp&w=3840&q=90&dpl=dpl_HsAF9iYD6c2mkxKrYJ6DfPhs1zN7)

KalingaStone and Caesarstone are both quality engineered stone brands; the practical differences are price, range breadth and supply. Caesarstone is the premium imported quartz label with prices to match. KalingaStone spans quartz, engineered marble and terrazzo in one catalogue, undercuts premium imports on like-for-like looks, and ships from UAE stock. For most projects the decision is budget and breadth, not quality.

## The two brands in one minute

Caesarstone is the veteran of engineered quartz: an Israeli-founded global brand that effectively created the premium quartz category, distributed in the UAE through its appointed dealer network and specified wherever the label itself carries weight. Its catalogue is quartz and adjacent porcelain surfaces, positioned and priced as the category's premium.

KalingaStone is Classic Marble Company's engineered stone brand, produced on Italian SIMEC lines at the Silvassa plant and exported to 66 countries. Its distinguishing structure is breadth: quartz across seven series, engineered marble, and block terrazzo under one name, 128 shades in the UAE catalogue, positioned as value engineering that competes upward on quality. In this market it is stocked as full slabs at Al Sajaa, Sharjah by Global Classic (/kalingastone), an authorized reseller for the Northern Emirates. Both brands make excellent surfaces; the rest of this comparison is about where each earns its price.

## Quartz against quartz: material and looks

Slab to slab, both brands make dense, non-porous engineered quartz around 90 percent mineral content, and both behave identically in the kitchen: no sealing, effortless stain resistance, the same trivet rule near heat. Certifications tell the same story, with KalingaStone's NSF food-safety listing and Microban antimicrobial options matching the hygiene case premium imports lead with.

On looks, Caesarstone's designer collections earned their reputation, particularly in the veined premium territory. KalingaStone's 69-shade quartz range covers the same ground, from pure whites through Carrara-look scattering to bold Calacatta-look veining, plus the 3300 by 2000 millimetre superjumbo format that decides whether an island needs a joint. At conversation distance, matched shades from either brand read as peers; specifiers choosing between them on a sample table are usually choosing between patterns, not quality tiers. The honest test is the slab viewing (/kalingastone/quartz), which is where label loyalty tends to meet budget reality.

## Range breadth: where KalingaStone pulls ahead

Caesarstone answers one question, quartz, superbly. KalingaStone answers three: the same engineered quartz territory, plus engineered marble, plus block terrazzo, in one coordinated catalogue. For a villa or hospitality project, that breadth is a practical advantage rather than a brochure line: quartz on the worktops, engineered marble across the floors and vanities, terrazzo in the lobby or wet areas, one supplier, one quality system, palettes chosen to sit together.

The engineered marble range matters most here, because premium quartz brands simply do not make it: 35 shades of slab-format marble at absorption under 0.1 percent, covered in the engineered marble guide (/blog/engineered-marble-complete-guide), owning the floors-and-walls territory where quartz is rarely specified. Terrazzo adds the third leg, five collections with fluted panels no quartz label offers. A Caesarstone project still needs a marble supplier and a terrazzo supplier; a KalingaStone project (/kalingastone) can be one phone call.

## Price: the honest AED comparison

Caesarstone in the UAE prices as the premium import it is: installed worktops commonly quote from the mid-hundreds of dirhams per square metre upward into four figures for designer collections, as of 2026 and varying by dealer and fabrication scope. KalingaStone quartz slabs supply at roughly AED 250 to 500 per square metre for standard series, with designer shades and superjumbo formats above the band, pricing that undercuts like-for-like premium import looks, often substantially at project volume.

The gap is brand positioning, not hidden corners: both are quality engineered stone, and the daily-performance comparison above is a draw. What the Caesarstone premium buys is the label and its specification cachet; what the KalingaStone saving funds is more project, more slabs, better fittings, or simply margin. BOQ-scale buyers feel the delta hardest, forty kitchens multiply every dirham of rate. The full KalingaStone pricing guide (/blog/kalingastone-price-uae) frames all three materials in AED; matched quotes against a specific scope settle it precisely.

## Supply in the UAE: stock, slabs, service

Both brands are legitimately supplied in the UAE, through different structures. Caesarstone runs through its appointed distributor with showroom presence and the ordering rhythms of an import brand. KalingaStone in the Northern Emirates runs through Global Classic's Al Sajaa warehouse: full slabs of all three materials physically on racks, viewable and taggable before purchase, quoted in AED usually within one working day, delivered to every emirate, with restock from the Silvassa plant in roughly four to six weeks for unstocked shades.

For project buyers the practical questions are the usual four: can I see my exact slabs, what is the lead time from stock, who stands behind fabrication tolerances, and what happens if a slab arrives damaged. Ask them of any supplier, ours included; the supplier-vetting guide (/blog/marble-slab-supplier-uae-how-to-choose) explains why traders go quiet on the fourth. Between two good brands, supply certainty and slab access are frequently the deciding vote, and they are exactly what a stocked warehouse exists to answer.

## Questions, answered.

###
Is KalingaStone as good as Caesarstone?

On daily performance, yes: both are dense engineered quartz with the same non-porous, no-sealing behaviour, and KalingaStone carries NSF and Microban options. The premium buys the label more than the material.

###
Which is cheaper?

KalingaStone, on like-for-like looks: standard series supply around AED 250 to 500 per square metre as slabs as of 2026, below comparable premium import quotes. At project volume the gap compounds.

###
Does Caesarstone make engineered marble or terrazzo?

No. Its catalogue is quartz and porcelain surfaces. KalingaStone spans quartz, engineered marble and terrazzo, which is the structural difference for whole-project supply.

###
Where can I compare both in person?

View KalingaStone as full slabs at Al Sajaa, Sharjah, and Caesarstone at its dealer showrooms. Seeing matched shades side by side is worth more than any comparison table, including this one.
